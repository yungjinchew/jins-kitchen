Pork, Fennel & Lemon Ragù with Pappardelle

Yield: 3–4 servings  ·  Total: ~3½ hr  ·  Active: ~55–60 min  ·  Oven: 300°F / 150°C
A bright, aromatic white ragù built around pork shoulder, fennel, lemon, and Pecorino Romano — tender shredded pork against soft pappardelle, with fennel fronds providing aromatic lift at service.

Ingredients

Ragù
Pancetta, finely chopped4 oz / 115 g
Onion, finely chopped1 large
Fennel bulbs (1½ large), finely chopped — fronds reserved1½ large
Garlic cloves, minced4 cloves
Fresh thyme, minced2 tsp
Diamond Crystal kosher salt1½ tsp
Black pepper, freshly ground1 tsp
Boneless pork shoulder, trimmed, cut into 2 pieces1½ lb / 680 g
Dry white wine150 ml / ⅔ cup
Chicken stock1 cup / 240 ml
Water1¼ cups / 300 ml
Finish
Lemon zest (2 lemons) — 75% into sauce, 25% reserved for plating2 lemons
Fresh lemon juice¼ cup / 60 ml
Heavy cream⅓ cup / 80 ml
Pecorino Romano, finely grated — 70% sauce, 30% table2 oz / 55 g
Pasta
Dried pappardelle12 oz / 340 g
Diamond Crystal kosher salt (pasta water)1 tbsp

Prep Phase

  1. Finely chop onion and fennel bulb.
  2. Mince garlic and thyme.
  3. Finely chop fennel fronds; reserve separately.
  4. Zest and juice the lemons separately. Divide zest: reserve 25% for plating; set aside 75% for the sauce.
  5. Pat pork completely dry with paper towels.

Active Cooking

Dutch oven runs two sequential fond builds before deglazing — transfer base mixture to a bowl between phases so the pan is clear for the pork sear.

Phase 1 — Build the Base

  1. Heat Dutch oven over medium-high heat.
  2. Add pancetta and ⅔ cup / 160 ml water. Cook until water evaporates and pancetta renders, 8–10 min.
  3. Continue until golden fond develops on the base.
  4. Add onion and fennel. Cook until softened and lightly browned, 7–10 min.
  5. Add garlic, thyme, salt, and pepper. Cook 30 seconds.

Transfer mixture to a bowl.

Phase 2 — Moderate Pork Sear

  1. Return Dutch oven to medium-high heat.
  2. Confirm pork is completely dry before adding to pan.
  3. Sear pork pieces until lightly browned on all sides. Develop moderate color only — avoid a dark crust.

Remove pork.

Phase 3 — Deglaze and Braise

  1. Add wine. Scrape up fond and reduce by roughly half.
  2. Return vegetable mixture. Add chicken stock and water.
  3. Return pork. Bring to a simmer.
  4. Cover and transfer to oven. Cook at 300°F / 150°C for approximately 2½ hours.

Doneness: pork shreds easily with light fork pressure; muscle groups separate without resistance.

Phase 4 — Develop the Sauce

  1. Remove pork to a plate. Cover Dutch oven and let stand 10–15 min so sidewall fond softens.
  2. Scrape all fond into the sauce.
  3. Shred pork into bite-sized pieces. Return pork and accumulated juices to the Dutch oven.

Phase 5 — Build Final Flavor

  1. Stir in 75% of the lemon zest and all of the lemon juice. Bring to a gentle simmer.
  2. Assess consistency.

Target: ragù should appear slightly too loose — almost stew-like. If it already heavily coats a spoon or sits in thick ridges, loosen with a splash of stock or water before proceeding.

Phase 6 — Cook Pasta

  1. Bring 4 quarts / 3.8 L water to a boil. Add 1 tbsp kosher salt.
  2. Cook pappardelle until al dente — typically 30–60 seconds beyond package timing for dried.
  3. Reserve 2 cups / 475 ml pasta water. Drain.

Phase 7 — Finish

Keep Dutch oven over low heat throughout this phase.

  1. Add pasta to the ragù.
  2. Add 1 cup / 240 ml reserved pasta water.
  3. Add 70% of the Pecorino. Toss until fully emulsified.
  4. Assess consistency — add additional pasta water if needed.
  5. Stir in heavy cream. Toss 30–60 seconds.
  6. Final consistency check — add additional pasta water if required.

Target: sauce coats pasta fluidly and pools lightly at the bottom of the bowl. It should not cling heavily.

Holding & Assembly

  1. Remove from heat. Rest 1–2 minutes only.
  2. Divide among warm bowls.
  3. Garnish with reserved lemon zest and fennel fronds.
  4. Serve remaining Pecorino at the table.

Serve immediately.

Critical Control Points

Severity Failure Mode & Cue Recovery
Critical Sauce over-reduced before pasta enters — sits in thick ridges and barely flows when spoon is dragged through it Loosen with stock, water, or pasta water before adding pasta
Critical Pork under-braised — resists shredding or pulls into long tough strands Return to oven until it shreds easily with light fork pressure
Major Moderate sear exceeded — dark crust and deeply roasted fond Proceed; dish remains serviceable
Major Pecorino clumps — cheese forms strings rather than emulsifying Add warm pasta water gradually while tossing
Minor Lemon dominates — acidity overwhelms pork and fennel Adjust with pasta water before considering additional cream

Adjust: Add salt only after Pecorino is incorporated. Correct texture with pasta water, not cream — pasta water is the primary finishing lever throughout. Increase richness with additional Pecorino before increasing cream. Use reserved lemon zest at plating for brightness; additional lemon juice only at service.
