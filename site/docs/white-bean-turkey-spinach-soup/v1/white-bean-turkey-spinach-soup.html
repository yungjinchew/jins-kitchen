Serves 3 adults · soup + adult salmon add-on for 2 · Total ~45 min · Active ~30 min

A soft white bean and turkey soup adapted for a post-extraction healing mouth: smooth and lukewarm for the son's portion, textured and brightened with lemon, herbs and chili at the table for the two adults, with a parallel roast salmon add-on to make it a full dinner. Lemony White Bean Soup with Turkey & Spinach

Yield: 3 adults · Total: ~45 min · Active: ~30 min · No oven (stovetop)

A soft, savory white bean and turkey soup with partially smashed beans for body; lemon, herbs and chili are moved entirely to the table so the shared base stays gentle on healing extraction sites.

Meal-level carve-out: the soup functions as a soft, single-register dish by design — no internal hard-texture contrast required, as the post-extraction context prohibits it.

Ingredients

Soup (shared base)
Olive oil3 tbsp / 45 ml
Onion, diced1 large
Carrot, peeled, diced1 large (~5 oz / 140 g)
Tomato paste1 tbsp
Ground cumin¾ tsp, plus more to taste
Ground turkey1 lb / 450 g
Garlic, minced3 cloves
Fresh ginger, finely grated1 tbsp
Diamond Crystal kosher salt¾ tsp, plus more to taste
Chicken stock1 qt / 950 ml
White beans (cannellini / great northern), drained & rinsed2 × 15 oz / 425 g cans
Frozen chopped spinach (default), thawed & squeezed5–6 oz / 140–170 g
Adult finishes — at the table, not in the pot
Lemon, juiced1
Soft herbs (parsley, dill, basil, chives), chopped1 cup
Olive oil, red-pepper flakes, black pepperto taste

Prep

  1. Dice onion and carrot. Carrot peeled — skin turns fibrous and works against the no-chew goal.
  2. Mince garlic; grate ginger.
  3. Drain and rinse beans; set aside about one-third for smashing.
  4. Thaw frozen spinach and squeeze out excess water firmly in a towel or sieve. If using fresh instead: 5 oz / 140 g baby spinach, roughly chopped.
  5. Chop herbs; hold for the table.

Active Cooking

  1. Heat a large pot over medium-high, 1 min. Add olive oil; heat until it thins, ~30 sec.
  2. Add onion and carrot. Sauté until very soft and browned at the edges, 7–10 min. Edges golden, onion translucent and collapsing.
  3. Add tomato paste and cumin. Sauté until the paste darkens and smells caramelized, ~1 min.
  4. Add turkey, garlic, ginger, and ¾ tsp salt. Break up with a spoon and cook through, 5–8 min. Primary cue: 165°F / 74°C on a probe. Corroborating: no raw pink remaining; fond building on pot base.
  5. Add stock and beans; bring to a simmer. Smash the reserved third of beans against the side of the pot and stir in to thicken. Simmer 15–20 min until thickened and flavorful.
  6. Add chopped spinach. Simmer until fully collapsed and silky, ~5 min. Fully wilted, no structure remaining.
  7. Off heat. Taste and adjust salt and cumin only — no lemon in the pot. Salt conservatively; the dish finishes at the table and the salmon carries its own seasoning.
  8. Son's portion (default — blended & sieved): ladle out 1–2 servings, blend with an immersion blender until silky, then pass through a fine sieve to remove bean skins and any turkey grit. Cool uncovered to lukewarm before serving.

Holding & Assembly

Soup is eaten lukewarm or at room temperature. Total time out of refrigeration — soup and salmon both — should not exceed 2 hours; refrigerate beyond that. The son's portion is served smooth and lukewarm-to-room-temperature, never hot — cool uncovered, stirring occasionally, until comfortable on the lip; no lemon, chili, or black pepper. Adult bowls are finished at the table with lemon, herbs, a thread of olive oil, and chili/pepper to taste, with salmon flaked on top.

Adjust: if the adult bowls read flat, it's almost always lemon or salt rather than more cumin — add lemon a teaspoon at a time off-heat. If the soup is too thick after holding, loosen with warm stock. The son's portion should taste mild and clean by design — correct only with a pinch of salt and a little stock to thin, nothing sharp.

Critical Control Points

SeverityFailure Mode & CueRecovery
Critical Ground turkey undercooked — below 165°F / 74°C, food-safety risk. Probe under 165°F / 74°C; meat soft-pink in the thickest clumps. Continue cooking until 165°F / 74°C throughout. Not served until reached.
Critical Son's portion served too hot — heat on raw extraction sites risks irritating the wound or dislodging the clot. Steam rising; warm-to-hot on the lip. Cool uncovered, stirring, until lukewarm-to-room-temperature. Do not serve until comfortable on the lip.

Roast Salmon Add-On

Yield: 2 adults (optional plain share for the son) · Total: ~25 min · Active: ~5 min · Oven: 275°F / 135°C

Gently roasted salmon pulled early for a soft, just-set flake, flaked over the two adult bowls to turn the soup into a complete dinner.

Ingredients

Salmon fillet, skin-on10 oz / 280 g
Olive oil1 tbsp / 15 ml
Diamond Crystal kosher salt½ tsp
Lemon wedges (adult-only), to serve—

Prep

  1. Pat fillet dry; rub with olive oil and ½ tsp salt.
  2. Hold at room temperature 15 min before roasting.

Active Cooking

  1. Heat oven to 275°F / 135°C.
  2. Place salmon skin-side down on a lined tray. Roast until it flakes with gentle pressure and is just opaque at the thickest point — pull at 120°F / 49°C internal; carries to ~125°F / 52°C off-heat. About 15–18 min for a 1-inch fillet. Flakes separate when nudged with a fork; center barely translucent at pull.
  3. Rest 5 min on the tray. Flake into large pieces, discarding skin.

Holding & Assembly

Adult-first add-on. Holds at room temperature within the same 2-hour out-of-fridge window. Flake over the two adult bowls at serving; lemon wedges are adult-only. Soft enough for the son if he'd like a little, kept plain and lemon-free — but flaky fish still needs some oral movement, so it's optional for him, not part of his core portion.

Adjust: if the salmon firms or dries while holding, fold the flaked pieces into the warm soup so the broth re-moistens them; this also softens it further for the son's optional share.

Critical Control Points

SeverityFailure Mode & CueRecovery
Major Salmon overcooked — pulled past 125°F / 52°C, flesh dries and firms, losing the soft flake the healing context needs. Flakes chalky, no sheen. Unrecoverable for texture; dish remains serviceable. Fold flaked salmon into the warm soup so the broth re-moistens it.
