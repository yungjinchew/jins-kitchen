Serves 3 adults
  • Maiale al Latte — pork shoulder braised in milk

Shared Prep

Lemon: 1 lemon total — zest in wide strips → Pork braise; remaining zest finely grated → Broccolini; juice → Pork sauce (1–2 tsp) + Broccolini squeeze.

Parsley: ¼ cup chopped → Beans (pinch reserved for the pork platter if desired).

Braising liquor: reserve ~3 tbsp of the pork's milk sauce before its final reduction → Beans.

If the window slips: the broccolini fails first — limp within ~10 min of dressing. Plate it last, serve immediately. Beans and pork both hold comfortably.

Workflow Notes

Critical path: The pork braise governs everything — it is the only long, fixed-duration component, and its doneness is a fork-feel judgment, not a clock. Everything else builds around its exit. If the pork needs more time at T−0:30, hold service: beans wait indefinitely on low, broccolini isn't started until T−0:15 anyway. Do not pull the pork early to hit a clock — under-braised shoulder is the meal's worst failure. Sacrifice timing, not doneness.

Oven & heat sequencing: The oven runs one job — pork at 300°F / 150°C, start to finish, no transitions. All other cooking is cooktop in the final 35 minutes. Induction #1 holds the sauce reduction, induction #2 runs the beans, and the broccolini chars on the outdoor gas stove (hot pan or direct burner, keeping high heat and splatter outside). To stay fully indoors, run broccolini on induction #2 in a hot cast-iron pan after the beans come off, holding the beans covered on low. Nothing competes for a heat source.

Maiale al Latte (Pork Shoulder Braised in Milk)

Yield: 3 adults · Total: ~3 hr (plus optional overnight salt) · Active: ~50 min · Oven: 300°F / 150°C

Pork shoulder browned hard and braised in milk with sage, garlic, and lemon zest until fork-soft, the milk reducing to a savory, curd-flecked sauce — meltingly soft meat against lightly granular, ricotta-like sauce solids.

Ingredients

Pork & Braise
Boneless pork shoulder, tied2 lb / 900 g
Diamond Crystal kosher salt (plus more to finish)~1¼ tsp
Black pepper, plus fresh grinds to finish½ tsp
Unsalted butter1 tbsp / 14 g
Olive oil1 tbsp / 15 ml
Whole milk3½ cups / 850 ml
Garlic, lightly smashed5 cloves
Fresh sage, whole leaves10
Lemon zest, wide strips (no pith)from 1 lemon
Bay leaf1
Hot water, as neededup to ~120 ml
Finish
Lemon juice, to taste1–2 tsp
Flaky saltto finish

Prep Phase

  1. Salt ahead (preferred): Pat the pork thoroughly dry, season all over with the salt and pepper, and refrigerate uncovered overnight (up to 24 hr) on a rack — penetrates seasoning, dries the surface for a better crust. No other day-before work. If not salting ahead: season and rest at room temperature 30–45 min while you prep.
  2. Pat the surface dry again if it came from the fridge. A dry surface is essential for the hard sear. Truss at 1-inch intervals if not already tied.
  3. Smash 5 garlic cloves. Pull 10 whole sage leaves from their stems — leave whole, do not chop (chopped sage disperses through the curds and muddies the sauce). Peel 1 lemon into wide zest strips, avoiding pith.

Active Cooking

  1. Brown the pork hard. In a heavy Dutch oven over medium-high (induction), heat butter and olive oil until the foam subsides. Brown on all sides, 12–15 min to a deep mahogany crust on every face. Few flavor-building ingredients here — this fond is the savory backbone, so push past a standard medium sear. Watch the base: deep brown is the goal, but lower the heat if it threatens to blacken — scorched fond turns the milk bitter. Transfer to a plate.
  2. Bloom aromatics. Lower to medium. Add garlic and sage to the fat; cook 60–90 sec until fragrant and the garlic is pale gold — not browned.
  3. Build the braise. Pour in the milk; add zest strips and bay. Scrape the fond off the base. Return the pork and juices. The milk should reach roughly halfway up the shoulder — top up toward the 850 ml if a wide pot leaves it short.
  4. Braise. Bring to a gentle simmer, then cover lid-ajar and transfer to the 300°F / 150°C oven. Braise ~2 hr 15 min, turning every 45–60 min so no face stays exposed long enough to dry. At 90 min: if milk is below ⅓ of meat height, add 60–120 ml hot water — not milk, which reads raw added late. The milk separates into golden curds in a thin whey — this is correct. Done when a fork twists with no resistance (secondary cue: 195–203°F / 90–95°C at the thickest part).
  5. Rest + remove aromatics + reduce. Lift the pork to a board, tent loosely. Fish out and discard the bay and zest strips now, before any reduction — left in during an active reduce, the zest over-extracts and citrus turns dominant. Then, if the sauce is thin, set over medium and reduce a few minutes to a loose, spoonable consistency.

Holding & Assembly

  1. The pork rests tented while you finish the sauce (rest overlaps the reduction — ~15 min total).
  2. Mash 2 softened garlic cloves into the sauce for body, then taste — braised cloves vary in strength, so add one more only if it reads thin on garlic. Mashing all five can tip the sauce sweet-garlicky, away from the clean pork-and-dairy register.
  3. Off-heat, stir 1–2 tsp lemon juice into the sauce; taste and adjust salt. Add juice only off the heat — boiling dulls brightness.
  4. Slice against the grain into thick slices (soft meat — sharp knife, gentle hand). Spoon curd sauce over and finish with fresh black pepper.

For acid, off-heat lemon juice is the main lever; add ½ tsp at a time if flat or heavy. For richness, more lemon cuts heaviness; a small knob of butter stirred in rounds out a lean sauce.

Creamy Cannellini Beans with Braising Liquor & Parsley

Yield: 3 as a side · Total: ~20 min · Active: ~15 min

Tinned cannellini gently simmered with garlic, shallot, and a spoonful of the pork's milk braising liquor, then partly mashed for a creamy, spoonable texture finished with parsley and olive oil — the carb-light alternative to mash.

Ingredients

Cannellini beans, drained and rinsed2 × 400 g tins (≈480 g)
Garlic, thinly sliced2 cloves
Shallot, finely diced1 small
Olive oil, plus extra-virgin to finish2 tbsp / 30 ml
Reserved pork braising liquor (before reduction)~3 tbsp
Water, plus more as needed~60 ml / ¼ cup
Flat-leaf parsley, chopped¼ cup / 15–20 g
Diamond Crystal kosher salt & black pepperto taste

Prep Phase

  1. Drain and rinse the beans well. Thinly slice 2 garlic cloves; finely dice 1 small shallot. Chop ¼ cup parsley.
  2. Reserve ~3 tbsp pork braising liquor before the pork sauce is reduced (at the pork's T−0:30 step) — reserving after reduction spoons over-concentrated sauce into the beans and skews seasoning.

Active Cooking

  1. Warm 2 tbsp olive oil in a saucepan over medium (induction). Add shallot and a pinch of salt; sweat 2–3 min until soft and translucent — not browned. Add garlic; cook 60 sec until fragrant and pale.
  2. Add beans, ~60 ml water, and the 3 tbsp reserved liquor. Bring to a gentle simmer; cook 10–12 min, stirring occasionally, until creamy and heated through.
  3. Mash approximately 20–25% of the beans against the pot to thicken into a loose, creamy base — leave the rest whole for texture. Stir in parsley off-heat, finish with a drizzle of extra-virgin olive oil and black pepper. Season to taste.

Holding & Assembly

  1. Hold covered on lowest heat up to ~30 min. Beans tighten as they sit; loosen with a splash of hot water or more liquor to a spoonable consistency before serving.

Critical Control Points

SeverityFailure Mode & CueRecovery
MinorBeans too tight / pasty — over-mashed or over-reduced; gluey not creamy.Loosen off-heat with hot water or liquor a tablespoon at a time.
MinorGarlic scorched — sliced garlic browns and turns bitter at the sweat.Keep heat at medium, add garlic after shallot softens; if scorched, restart the aromatics.

Adjust: For salt, the liquor is already seasoned — salt last and lightly. For richness, a little more liquor deepens; water lightens if too heavy. Texture is the main lever — mash more for creamier, less for more intact beans.

Charred Broccolini with Lemon Zest

Yield: 3 as a side · Total: ~12 min · Active: ~10 min

Broccolini blanched briefly, then charred in a hot pan until blistered and tender-crisp, dressed with grated lemon zest, a squeeze of juice, and olive oil — the bright, firm, charred counterpoint to two soft, rich components.

Ingredients

Broccolini, trimmed (woody ends removed; thick stalks halved lengthwise)2 bunches / ~350 g
Olive oil, plus a little to finish1 tbsp / 15 ml
Lemon zest, finely gratedfrom shared lemon
Lemon juicea squeeze
Diamond Crystal kosher saltto taste
Chili flakes, optionala pinch

Prep Phase

  1. Trim the woody ends. Halve thick stalks lengthwise so they cook evenly with thinner ones. Finely grate the lemon zest.

Active Cooking

  1. Blanch: Drop the broccolini into well-salted boiling water for 90 sec, just until the stalks lose their raw snap. Drain and pat thoroughly dry. The blanch lets a fast, hot pan-sear blister the surface without leaving the stalks raw — a flat pan has no indirect zone to finish them gently, so they need par-cooking first. Toss with 1 tbsp olive oil and a pinch of salt.
  2. Char: Heat a cast-iron or heavy stainless pan over medium-high until properly hot (outdoor gas burner preferred for heat and splatter, or induction indoors). Lay the broccolini in a single layer — don't crowd — and char 3–4 min, turning, until blistered and lightly blackened in spots and the stalks are tender-crisp. Blistered and spotted, not ashed grey — pull while there's still bite. Char in two batches if crowded; piling it steams rather than blisters.

Holding & Assembly

  1. Do not hold — plate-last component. Off heat, dress immediately with grated zest, a squeeze of juice, a thread of olive oil, salt, and optional chili. Serve at once.

Critical Control Points

SeverityFailure Mode & CueRecovery
MajorOvercooked / limp — held too long after dressing or charred past tender-crisp; stalks floppy, no snap. Unrecoverable for texture; dish remains serviceable.None — texture is lost once gone. Prevent by charring to just-tender and serving immediately; this is why it's plated last.
MinorUndercooked stalk — char looks done but the thick stalk is raw-firm inside.Return to the pan briefly; mostly prevented by the blanch and halving thick stalks.

Adjust: For salt, dress and taste at the end — char concentrates flavor, so season lightly. For acid, lemon is the brightness lever; zest first, then juice a little at a time. Heat: chili flakes optional, at plating.
